# Property 3D from photographs and videos

The primary 3D workflow reconstructs captured surfaces using COLMAP camera recovery followed by gsplat GPU training. A hand-drawn floor plan and supplied furniture are not inputs or prerequisites. The manual architectural tool remains separately labelled and accessible with `view=manual-plan`.

Open an agent workspace with `view=reconstruction` or the existing `view=construction` link:

1. Upload all overlapping real photographs and room clips before preparing views. Photographs accept JPEG/PNG up to 10 MiB each; the existing room clip flow accepts bounded video uploads. Record each room and connecting doorway from multiple positions. Include sideways doorway views that show furniture or textured details in both adjoining spaces at once; moving only straight through a narrow door may leave separate room reconstructions. Keep a fixed lens within each clip.
2. Use **Prepare all photo and video views**. The interface waits for agency processing capacity. Sources remain private. Photo decoding corrects orientation, removes metadata and creates a maximum-1600-pixel view using the confined CPU decoder; video retains actual decoded timestamps.
3. Load prepared capture views. Give clips from distinct rooms different room labels; reuse the same label for clips inside one room, and leave shared hallway/doorway connecting clips blank. Saved labels and frame selections reload when the workspace is reopened. Use a source checkbox to exclude an entire poor clip, or individual frame checkboxes for blurred or inconsistent views. Labels guide image matching but supply no geometry or camera positions. Analyze the selected set. At least ten usable views are needed for camera recovery and held-out evaluation. Inspect exclusions and privacy masks, then start camera reconstruction.
4. When the camera report supplies calibrated training inputs, start **Reconstruct captured property**. The enabled private GPU runner trains and exports a scene. Inspect the actual scene and held-out renders before requesting independent privacy/render review.

All inputs and results retain their property scope and source revision. Uploading new sources requires fresh preparation and review. Photo sources receive separate calibration groups rather than assuming that different files share a lens. The current bounded camera profile accepts at most 32 capture groups. Video evaluation holds out temporal blocks and excludes neighbouring training views. Photo evaluation holds out distinct images by a deterministic content-hash order; it cannot certify independent viewpoints. Physical scale and room completeness remain unverified without additional evidence.

Reproduce with `sh scripts/demo/generate-property-capture.sh` against the local Compose development stack.

The local detailed investigation worker targets 6,000 steps with bounded gradient densification, up to 75,000 splats and a 1,024-pixel training edge. Camera recovery and GPU training each have a 20-minute native execution ceiling plus bounded transfer/startup time. Legacy 500/3,000-step fixed-density results remain readable. The private preview defaults to the tier with the most detail and offers recovered capture viewpoints for inspection. It starts from a recovered camera in its calibrated aspect ratio, preferring a held-out view with the highest measured PSNR. This is inspection framing, not verified room navigation or scale. Source authority is rechecked against the exact training archive.

A gsplat scene represents visible surfaces and furnishings. It does not automatically segment each furniture item into an editable CAD object or infer hidden walls. This generated example demonstrates the pipeline; it does not establish accuracy for a real address. Earlier desk references remain historical evidence. Public listings and VR remain unchanged; VR is paused.
